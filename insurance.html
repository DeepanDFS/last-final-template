Buying Guide

How to choose the right insurance

A short walkthrough of what actually matters when picking a policy.

For term/life insurance, a common starting point is 10–15 times your annual income. For health insurance, factor in your city's typical hospitalization costs and family size rather than picking the cheapest sum insured.
A lower premium often means lower coverage or more exclusions. Compare the coverage-to-premium ratio, not the premium in isolation.
Health plans typically have an initial waiting period (commonly 30 days) plus a longer waiting period for pre-existing conditions (2–4 years). Shorter waiting periods usually mean a higher premium.
Every policy excludes certain conditions or scenarios. Read the exclusions list before you buy — this is where most claim disputes come from.
Check whether the insurer offers cashless claims at your preferred hospitals or garages, and review their claim settlement ratio — a good indicator of how reliably they pay out.
Look at renewal conditions (does the premium jump sharply with age or claims?) and whether useful add-ons — like zero depreciation for motor or a critical illness rider for term — are available and reasonably priced.
